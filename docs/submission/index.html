Project description

Kitty helps people who already trust each other save in a rotating circle. Everyone agrees to contribute a set amount each round, and members take turns receiving the pot.

Organizing a circle can mean chasing messages, comparing payment confirmations and keeping a separate record. Kitty puts the agreed rules, contributions and payout history into one experience. The organizer invites friends by link. Before joining, each member sees the deposit, payout order and missed-payment terms. The contracts hold the funds rather than the organizer's personal account.

The Android app uses Mera passkeys, so members sign in with a supported device's fingerprint or screen lock. Gas is sponsored on testnet. During an unlocked session, a contribution can be signed under the app's session policy; creating or joining a circle and sending money require fresh confirmation.

Eligible members can make a sealed offer to receive a pot earlier in exchange for giving up part of it. The discount is split between credits for other members and the shared pool. Actual payouts can also reflect holdback and arrears. A missed contribution can draw on a member's deposit and available pool funds; that protection is finite, not a guarantee. Members remain responsible for future contributions after receiving their turn.

Each account has a cat that reflects its circle standing. Kept commitments, misses and defaults affect the record and future circle terms; the app explains those terms in words. Decorative interactions with the cat do not change the financial rules.

Kitty also includes test-dollar sending and conversion through Agora's testnet Instant Settlement pair. Envio HyperIndex supplies circle history, account records and restoration data. Same-passkey restoration depends on supported passkey access and the relevant network services; it is not recovery from losing the passkey itself.

This is a hackathon prototype on Monad testnet. AUSD and the cash-out stand-in token have no cash value here, yield is simulated where enabled, and no production fiat withdrawal is provided. Rounds close when an open member app or a running keeper submits the close; a hosted keeper is not currently running. A real-money release remains future work.

First users and how we will reach them

Our first research participants are existing organizers and small groups of friends who want a regular saving commitment and an individual turn to receive the pot. We will recruit through people we can reach directly, including friends from different backgrounds. Nationality is not the segment; the relevant behavior is coordinating repeated contributions with people you already know.

We will interview 5–10 adults across two or three groups, including organizers and members, before claiming demand. We will ask about their most recent real experience: how they collected contributions, decided the order, handled missed payments and kept records. A group that only needs expense reimbursement or a common purchase may be better served by its existing tools.

Next, we will run clearly labeled testnet practice circles. We will measure whether participants can explain the deposit and ongoing obligations, join without help, identify the next payment and recipient, and complete and collect from a circle. We will report denominators and distinguish real participants from scripted test accounts. An accelerated rehearsal will not be described as long-term retention.

Our initial distribution experiment is organizer-led: help one organizer explain a practice circle to their own group, then observe whether they choose to organize another. We will compare Kitty against chat plus a spreadsheet and existing payment tools. Invitations are a way to reach a group, not evidence that a referral loop already works.

The initial message is clear terms and a shared record. We do not promise financial returns, guaranteed coverage or lower end-to-end transfer costs. Monetization is unvalidated; optional organizer tools are one later hypothesis. We have not selected or established eligibility for a real-money launch jurisdiction. Such a release would need appropriate review, security work and supported funding and withdrawal routes.

This is a research plan, not a claim that these interviews or growth results have already happened. Background evidence and competitor comparisons are in the research brief.

Steps for a judge to try Kitty

Kitty uses test dollars on Monad testnet; do not deposit real money. The current build is Android-only. Use a supported Android phone with a screen lock and passkey provider such as Google Password Manager.

1. Open https://kitty-circle.vercel.app/download on the phone and install the APK. Android may ask you to allow installs from that browser.

2. Open Kitty, choose a display name, select the applicable country option, confirm you are 18 or older, and create an account with your passkey. Choose “Somewhere else” if your country is not listed. The country field does not enable local payment rails. Let the app obtain sponsored gas and test dollars. Name your cat if you want to.

3. With two friends or explicitly labeled test participants, create Friday Circle. Choose $5 per round, Every 10 min, In 30 min, and offers off for the first practice. Keep deposits earning off. Review the settings before confirming. The product supports 2–12 members; three makes this demonstration easy to follow.

4. Send each participant their own invite link. They open it, review their deposit and payout terms, and join using independent accounts before joining closes. Do not share passkeys or invite secrets publicly.

5. Each member pays the contribution shown for the current round. Keep a member's app open on the circle, or run the keeper, when the round is ready to close. The next recipient and actual payout follow the rules. A payout can be smaller than the gross pot because of holdback or debt; inspect the app's amounts.

6. Repeat until the circle finishes, then collect the remaining eligible balances. Read the circle's activity and the Account record. The example's short windows are for testnet practice.

7. Optional: use Send with another participant's pay link to send test dollars and inspect the receipt. The “Ready to cash out” option converts into the testnet stand-in token. It does not withdraw to a bank or mobile-money account.

8. For a separate recovery test, first confirm continued access to the same passkey on a test account. Clearing app storage is a destructive local step: do it only for that prepared test account. Sign in again and inspect which account, circles and encrypted details restore. A second-device test requires actual supported access to the same owner's passkey on that device; an animation or one-device reinstall is not that proof.

Agora: payments integration

Kitty combines rotating savings circles with peer-to-peer test-dollar sending. Agora's testnet AUSD is the contribution asset on Monad testnet.

Members create a passkey account, receive sponsored gas and request test AUSD from the faucet. A send can arrive as AUSD or use Agora's Instant Settlement pair to convert and deliver the output token to the recipient in one transaction. On Monad testnet, CTK is the stand-in for the settlement asset; the app's “Ready to cash out” label does not mean a real-world withdrawal is available.

The sending implementation is in app/src/money.ts. It obtains a quote, supplies a minimum output and a chain-clock deadline, and uses the recipient as the swap destination. Approval is bounded to the required amount rather than an open-ended grant. The receiving screen also provides conversion between the test balances. Send links use one-time keys so an unclaimed send can be recovered by its sender.

The app displays confirmation timing on the receipt. Any performance result in our submission must come from the actual captured test with its start/end definition, build, device, network and transaction hash. We do not equate chain execution cost with the complete cost of funding, exchange and local withdrawal.

The present user value is being able to coordinate a circle and follow a test-dollar send in the same interface. Our first discovery work targets existing organizers and trusted friend groups. Production funding and cash-out partners, market eligibility, operating costs and a business model are future validation work. We do not infer legal exemption from using contracts or charging no fee.

Evidence to attach: native passkey onboarding, AUSD balance, completed send/receive, recipient balance and linked transaction. Testnet faucet funding is not a fiat on-ramp; browser illustrations are not transaction evidence.

Mera: account and session experience

Mera provides Kitty's passkey account layer. A supported WebAuthn PRF output is used for account derivation, and Mera's signing session signs transactions. The app's adapter lives in app/src/account/passkey.ts.

After onboarding, Kitty obtains sponsored gas, requests test AUSD and prepares the test settlement account. The onboarding notice reports the observed setup timing. The number of authenticator ceremonies can vary with device support, so we will report the actual count and timing from a retained native capture rather than assume a one-prompt outcome.

Kitty supplies its own session policy in app/src/policy.ts and app/src/session.tsx. Actions already constrained by the accepted circle rules, including paying a contribution and revealing an offer, can proceed during an unlocked session. Creating or joining a circle, sending, conversion, send-link operations and paying external debt require fresh confirmation. This is an app-enforced policy, not an onchain smart-account spending limit.

The session ends after five minutes in the background, thirty minutes idle or an explicit Lock action. Readable screens can remain available; the next action that requires signing asks for an unlock. Automatic revealing or round closing depends on the relevant app being open, unlocked and connected within the required window.

Restoration uses the same supported passkey for the account and derived encryption keys, Envio for circle membership/history, and encrypted storage for the profile and roster data. It does not recreate a lost passkey. Service availability and access to that passkey matter. A wiped-app test and a genuine second-device test are distinct pieces of evidence.

The composition is Mera plus sponsored gas, Agora test-dollar flows, passkey-derived encrypted state and Envio-backed history. Evidence should show onboarding to the first confirmed transaction, an unprompted permitted action, a freshly confirmed sensitive action, Lock/unlock and the actual restore sequence.

Mera: One Passkey, Many Keys

Kitty uses a passkey-derived root for more than transaction signing. Domain-separated HKDF-SHA256 derivations in app/src/account/keys.ts give each purpose its own namespace and relevant chain, circle, round or seat context.

Bid salts let the app commit to an offer without publishing its value. On restore, it can derive the salt again and search the bounded discount range against the onchain commitment. A supported second device with the same passkey can perform that derivation, but revealing still has to occur inside the reveal window.

Per-seat invite keys authorize a particular circle seat. A roster key encrypts the circle title and member names with authenticated encryption; a separate wrapping key protects each member's stored roster key. The organizer can rederive unused invite capabilities. Profile encryption and lookup identifiers have separate derivations. Send-link keys support one-time transfer addresses and recovery of unclaimed sends.

These are capabilities and encrypted data, not a claim that all secrets stay on one phone. Invite and send links deliberately carry sensitive capabilities in their fragments. The root secret is not uploaded, but links must remain private and encrypted copies can be stored for recovery.

The repository contains shared cryptographic vectors and tests for the derivations and chain-facing commitments. Authenticated encryption binds stored data to its intended context. This supports repeatable restoration without relying only on a device cache; it does not replace a security audit.

For the cross-device demonstration, retain a genuine capture of one device sealing an offer and a second supported device, controlled by the same tester, restoring and revealing it while the original device is inactive. If that evidence has not been captured, describe the derivation and leave the live criterion unclaimed. Three friends should keep independent accounts; they do not share a Google account to simulate this test.

Envio: the history behind the interface

Kitty uses Envio HyperIndex to turn contract activity into the history a member needs: circles joined, payments, payouts, missed contributions, account standing and keepsakes. It also supplies circle membership for restoration after signing in again.

The configuration covers Monad testnet, chain 10143, for Kitty activity and Monad mainnet, chain 143, for earnAUSD reference data. The mainnet reference rate is separate from the simulated yield paid by the testnet stand-in vault. We do not present a stale rate or event count as a current live result.

Factory events dynamically register circle clones. Participating accounts are registered so token-transfer processing can identify relevant money movement. The schema contains event-level records and derived views such as the activity feed, account aggregates, distinct counterparties, keepsakes, house progress and yield-rate observations. Reorg handling is enabled in the configuration.

The Android app reads through https://kitty-circle.vercel.app/api/graphql, a proxy that constrains queries and decouples the app from the underlying deployment URL. Live financial actions still read the chain; indexer availability is particularly important to history and restoration. This is not an offline payment system.

The repository includes config.yaml, schema.graphql, handlers and tests under indexer/. services/src/demo/watch.ts observes when events become queryable. A strong evidence clip shows one actual contribution, its transaction/block, the matching indexed record and the same event in the app. Record the observation method; polling delay is part of the watcher's measurement.

Evidence to attach: current Envio Cloud deployment and sync status, both configured chain sources, public repository access and a matching captured event. A terminal replay must be labeled as recorded evidence. The motion explainer by itself proves none of these live conditions.
