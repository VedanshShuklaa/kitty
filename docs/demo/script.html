02 / Words, pictures, timing

Friends. A circle.
Something to look forward to.

A complete camera-free story for Kitty, built around people you actually know. No actors, invented hometowns or nationality roles. Use animated cats, clear type and the real interface.

Revised 6 October 2026 · Creative scripts, not a record of a completed demo

The new story

Kitty helps friends save together in a rotating circle. Everyone agrees to contribute each round; each round, one member takes a turn receiving the pot. The app makes the terms, payments and history easier to follow. This is about an existing group’s commitment to one another, not splitting a restaurant bill or building a shared spending balance.

Our example is Friday Circle: you and two friends, with three cats as visual companions. Use the participants’ own preferred names with permission. They can contribute screenshots or a short voice line asynchronously. Nobody needs to dress up, show their face, pretend to live somewhere else or share a personal account.

One simple line: “Save together. Take turns. Keep your circle in the loop.”
Product descriptor: “A rotating savings circle for friends.”
Footer on financial scenes: “Monad testnet · test tokens · no cash value.”

The research supports testing an organizer-led, trusted-group use case. It does not establish demand, a global launch entitlement or a market-size estimate for Kitty. Keep broad financial statistics out of the short demo; the product story is stronger when the audience can understand one circle.

PeopleHost, Friend A, Friend B. These are editing labels; replace with real, consenting names. A scripted seat is labeled “Test participant.” Kitty supports 2–12 members; three is the recommended demo size.
CircleFriday Circle. “Friday” is its name, not a claim that the app has a special Friday-only schedule.

Round story: everyone pays round 1; Host receives the first payout. In round 2, Friend A pays and seals an offer; Friend B deliberately misses in this test scenario. Friend A’s valid revealed offer can win; Friend B’s deposit covers the planned single miss. In round 3, all remaining contributions are paid and Friend B’s payout first settles their covered arrears. Everyone collects their remaining eligible balance when the circle finishes. Check the receipts; do not hardcode the net payout.

Economics notes for the editor

The 15-token figure is the gross pot, not the amount immediately spendable by the recipient. At 10%, the example discount is 1.50 if all 15 tokens are funded. With the app’s default 10% pool share of the discount, 0.15 goes to the pool and 1.35 becomes credits divided among the other non-defaulted members. Two such members receive 0.675 each. Held-back funds, arrears and other debt can reduce the immediate payout further.

A joining deposit can be used to cover missed payments; it is not a fee or an unconditional refund. Holdback is money retained from an early payout and released under the circle’s rules. Deposits and the shared pool can run short, so do not say every miss is guaranteed to be covered. Petting a cat is decorative; it does not increase standing. Standing is based on circle activity and can affect the terms offered in future circles.

Five people are optional only after five real or explicitly labeled test accounts are provisioned and eligible. Do not animate five contributors and then show a three-person transaction as its proof.

What changed from the older scripts

The new story removes the invented regional family, collector-crime opening, regional jargon and country-by-country launch promises. The main hook is coordination among friends. It replaces a synchronized live shoot with reusable captures and motion composition; makes the deposit/holdback distinction explicit; keeps cat standing factual; and qualifies automatic closing, recoverability, test yield, cash-out and speed claims. Historical scripts are superseded by this page.
