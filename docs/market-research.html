Research brief · 6 October 2026

Start with a shared habit.
Build around people who already trust each other.

Kitty's clearest first audience is a small group that wants to make regular contributions and take turns receiving a pot. Nationality is not the segment. The job is coordinating a commitment without one friend becoming everyone's bookkeeper.

Decision: position Kitty as “Save together. Take turns. Keep the plan clear.” Show an ordinary group of friends, use their chosen names, and demonstrate a complete practice circle. Treat cross-border settlement as a technical capability to prove separately.

Desk research completed 6 October 2026. Ten primary sources. Product grounding: the local README and existing demo/submission documents. No new customer interviews, market-size estimates or live payment benchmarks were conducted.

01 · What the evidence supports

A broad problem; a specific product hypothesis

Saving, remembering commitments and coordinating money with other people are useful starting points for a global story. They do not establish that everyone wants a rotating circle, a dollar-denominated balance or an onchain account. The evidence below supports exploring those questions; it does not answer them for Kitty.

Observed evidence and the boundary of each claim

Financial access is already widespreadThe World Bank's 2025 release reports that nearly 80% of adults have a financial account. It also reports that 40% of adults in developing economies saved in an account in 2024, up 16 percentage points since 2021. World Bank, 16 July 2025.People with bank accounts may still face coordination problems. Account ownership or the unbanked population is not a count of prospective Kitty customers.

Savings pressure also exists in a high-income marketIn the Federal Reserve's 2025 US survey, 63% said they would cover a hypothetical $400 expense using cash or its equivalent; 12% said they could not cover it by any means. “Equivalent” includes a credit card paid in full at the next statement. Federal Reserve, May 2026.The remaining 37% are not all unable to pay. Financial pressure does not prove demand for a group commitment; it may make additional locked funds unsuitable.
Feedback can support a savings habitTwo experiments among Chilean microcredit clients found increased deposits and balances from a group savings treatment and from feedback messages. Transaction volume, wallet addresses and a circle's recycled contributions cannot be counted as unique customers or revenue.
Research conclusion: build the pitch around understandable coordination and a visible payment record. Keep “saving more,” “lower transfer costs” and “less organizer work” as hypotheses until Kitty measures them. A beautiful animation demonstrates an idea; a participant completing a task demonstrates usability.

03 · Who to learn from first

Choose by behavior, access and need

Proposed segment priorities. These are research hypotheses, not measured market rankings.

Defer · Strangers and urgent borrowingOpen public circles or people needing immediate emergency liquidity.These introduce underwriting, fraud, affordability and support questions beyond the demonstrated prototype.A cat's standing is treated as a guarantee of repayment, or participation depends on money being accessible at any moment.

A practical demo group: you and two willing friends can play Organizer, Member 2 and Member 3. Use their real chosen names or neutral aliases such as Alex, Sam and Jo. Let the app show an accurate country selection if a country must be chosen; do not manufacture residence, accents, clothes, hometowns or a shared family history. Their nationalities need no explanation in the film.

A three-person demo is a story and a technical rehearsal. A scripted crew member is automation. Neither establishes adoption. Label automated participants as demo accounts and reserve “user” metrics for consenting humans who actually completed the defined action.

04 · A clearer story

Explain the money before the mechanism

Suggested opening

“Three friends. One simple plan. Each contributes the same amount each round, and they take turns receiving the pot. Kitty keeps the schedule, the rules and the record in one place.”

Then show three avatars, three contributions and one recipient. Repeat with the next recipient. The demonstration uses test dollars on Monad testnet. Any deposit, offer or holdback shown must use the actual terms displayed by the app.

Claims to use in scripts and the proof they require
Use this wordingRequired evidence or boundary
“A savings circle for people who trust each other.”Define equal contributions and rotating individual payouts. A country-neutral explanation does not establish product availability in every country.
“The contract holds the circle's test funds and applies its rules.”Avoid “nobody holds the money.” Explain deposits, shared-pool limits and any remaining shortfall; coverage is conditional, not an insurance guarantee.
“A due round closes when the app or a keeper submits the transaction.”The repository documents no hosted keeper. Prove the actual close transaction; do not imply a timer alone executes a smart contract.
“Sign in with a supported passkey. Gas is sponsored in this prototype.”Show the actual Android flow. Recovery needs the same available passkey and a compatible provider/device; it is not guaranteed recovery from every lost phone.
“An onchain test transfer and conversion through Agora.”Show a transaction receipt. A UI label saying “Ready to cash out” represents testnet settlement assets here, not a completed bank or mobile-money payout.
“Your cat reflects your circle record.”Explain which events change standing. It is not an external credit rating, proof of character or promise that another member will pay.
“Names are encrypted; transaction activity is public.”Do not call the whole product anonymous or completely private. Avoid displaying real sensitive information in a public demo.
“Completed in this measured test.”State the test date and conditions for any timing. Do not turn one successful send into a universal speed, cost or reliability claim.

Suggested information order: people and plan → contribution → next turn → visible history → one exception → the technology that made it possible. Keep bidding, shared-pool accounting and recovery in short supporting sequences. Launching with every feature at once makes it harder to tell whether viewers understood the core product.

Creative implication: use motion graphics for the explanation and actual captured product states for evidence. A simple three-avatar arrangement communicates the idea without a room, camera or cast on location. Keep an “Illustration” or “Sample data” label on recreated interfaces; use “Recorded on Monad testnet” only for a genuine capture. The production guide turns this into a repeatable AI-assisted workflow.

05 · A two-week learning plan

Five to ten people can expose the next decision

Recruit 5–10 adults across 2–3 existing groups. Include organizers and members, at least two people outside the immediate friend group, and a mix of familiarity with digital wallets. This is qualitative discovery and usability work. It cannot estimate market demand, prove financial outcomes or compare cultures statistically.

A modest schedule; dates are relative to the start of research
WhenActionArtifact to save
Days 1–2Recruit and schedule short calls. Say clearly that this is a test-token prototype study, with no investment or financial return.A consented participant list using aliases; current tools and organizing experience.

A 25-minute interview you can run without a camera

  1. 0–2 min · Set expectations. “We are testing a testnet prototype, not asking you to deposit money. You can skip any question. I will take notes; is that okay?” Ask separately before recording audio or screen content.
  2. 2–7 min · Understand the last real attempt. “Tell me about the last time your group collected money repeatedly. What was it for? Who kept track? What happened after someone paid?” Ask for one concrete example, not an opinion about the concept.
  3. 7–11 min · Find the friction. “When was it confusing or inconvenient? What did you do? How often did you remind people? What worked well?” Record actual effort if remembered; do not convert guesses into measured hours.
  4. 11–14 min · Check the job. “Did everyone need their own payout, or were you paying for one shared thing? What decided the order? What happened if someone could not pay?” The answer may point away from rotating circles.
  5. 14–18 min · Show the simple explanation. Use the three-person illustration. Ask: “Explain what happens to the money in your own words. After you receive the pot, do you still contribute? What is the deposit for? When can you access it?” Do not correct the first answer before recording it.
  6. 18–22 min · Observe one task. Ask the participant to identify their next payment and payout order in the app or clearly labeled preview. Record success unaided, success with help or failure. If using sample data, do not record a simulated click as an onchain transaction.
  7. 22–25 min · Test tradeoffs. “Which would you choose for that real situation: your current method, a shared bank pot, or this? What would stop you? Who would need to agree?” Ask whether they want a practice follow-up; willingness to pay remains unvalidated.

Decision rules to set before interviews

These are proposed local thresholds, not industry benchmarks. Continue with this segment if at least three independent participants describe a recent recurring problem and at least two organizers choose a second practice circle without being repeatedly chased. Rework the explanation if more than one participant mistakes a payout for extra income or believes they can stop contributing afterward. Fix any confusion about deposits or test money before using the film as onboarding material.

For every test circle, record invite acceptance, successful join, first contribution, payout comprehension, round completion, collection and organizer support minutes. Report counts with denominators—“4 of 6 completed unaided”—and separate human participants from crew accounts. A complete accelerated rehearsal does not establish month-to-month retention or real-money repayment.

Useful counter-test: show a simple shared ledger beside Kitty. Ask which job each solves. If most people choose the ledger and cannot name a benefit worth the extra commitment, improve the use case before investing in a larger launch.

06 · Size what you can actually reach

Use a recruitment funnel before a market-size slide

No defensible dollar TAM for Kitty follows from the sources reviewed. A serviceable market would require evidence about eligible jurisdictions, funding routes, device access, groups with the right saving behavior and willingness to adopt. Multiplying the world's savers by an arbitrary subscription price would disguise those unknowns.

An illustrative planning model. Every number below is an assumption, not traction or a forecast.
StepIllustrative arithmeticReplace with observed data
Reach20 organizers contacted through consenting introductions.Number reached; number with a relevant recent problem.
Activation20 × assumed 40% = 8 completed practice circles.Groups completing a full cycle, not accounts created.
Participation8 circles × assumed 5 seats = 40 seat participations.Unique humans and repeat participation separately. One person may occupy seats in several circles.
Repeat8 × assumed 25% = 2 organizers starting another circle.Second-circle starts and completions, with observation window.
Revenue hypothesisA hypothetical $5 monthly organizer plan × 8 paying organizers = $40 gross monthly revenue.Actual willingness to pay, legal feasibility and service cost. There is no paid plan validated here.

Distribution experiment: recruit one organizer at a time through the team's actual network. Give them a short explanation and a labeled testnet invitation. Observe whether they can explain it to their group without the founder attending. Ask for further introductions only after a completed practice cycle; invitations are a feature, not proof of a self-sustaining referral loop.

Costs to measure: sponsored transactions, RPC and indexer usage, hosting, customer support time, recovery assistance and abuse handling. A future service also needs a budget for security review and applicable legal, operational and payment-partner requirements. Sponsored gas is paid by someone, even when the participant does not see a fee.

Revenue to explore later: optional organizer administration features may be easier to explain than taking a cut of every contribution. Compare what organizers would pay with the cost of supporting their group. Do not call participant principal, shared-pool balances or testnet transaction volume revenue. Do not present yield or a future partner commission as an existing business model.

What a real-money decision would need

Choose a specific jurisdiction and operating model only after understanding demand and obtaining qualified review of the proposed service, contracts, payment routes and consumer obligations. Geography-neutral branding does not settle those questions. Nationality is also not the same as residence or service eligibility. This brief makes no conclusion about permission to launch in any country.

Before promising cross-border savings, measure the complete user journey: local funding, exchange-rate spread, transfer costs, conversion, withdrawal, failure handling and the recipient's final usable amount. A fast testnet transaction demonstrates one part of that journey. Present the current build as a prototype until the rest is actually available and supported.
